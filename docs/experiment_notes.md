# Experiment Notes

This document defines the estimands, time splits, simulation settings, and
limitations for the committed outputs. The code in the repository generates
all reported values.

## 1. Parameterization

The rating coordinate and the natural Bradley–Terry coordinate are related by

$$
\theta=(\log 10/400)R.
$$

Consequently, the conventional Elo update factor $K$ and the constant SGD step
$\alpha$ satisfy

$$
\alpha=(\log 10/400)K.
$$

Pairwise probabilities are invariant under a common shift of all ratings. The
corresponding logistic objective is therefore considered on the centered
rating subspace, or after fixing an equivalent additive normalization. Elo's
zero-sum update preserves that normalization.

The code uses $K$ in Elo model interfaces and $\alpha$ in simulation output so
that the numerical results can be compared with stochastic-approximation
results.

## 2. Stationary Fluctuations

One focal player has fixed latent skill and faces a stream of opponents whose
ratings are observed. Outcomes are generated by the same Bradley–Terry model
used by the estimator. Each candidate step sees identical opponents and
outcomes.

Default settings:

| setting | value |
|---|---:|
| $K$ grid | 2, 4, 8, 16, 32 |
| games | 200,000 |
| burn-in | 40,000 |
| sample interval | 20 updates |
| seed | 20260819 |

The reported statistic is
$\mathrm{Var}(\widehat{\theta}-\theta^\star)/\alpha$. Standardized errors are
also compared with a Gaussian law in a QQ plot.

Let $X$ be the known opponent skill and
$p^\star=\sigma(\theta^\star-X)$. Up to an additive constant, the population
objective is

$$
H(\theta)
=\mathbb{E}\left[
\log\bigl(1+e^{\theta-X}\bigr)-p^\star(\theta-X)
\right].
$$

At the minimizer,

$$
H''(\theta^\star)
=\mathbb{E}\left[p^\star(1-p^\star)\right]
=A>0,
$$

so the local exponent in the paper is $m=2$. On the other hand,

$$
H'(\theta)\longrightarrow
\begin{cases}
1-\mathbb{E}[p^\star], & \theta\to+\infty,\\
-\mathbb{E}[p^\star], & \theta\to-\infty.
\end{cases}
$$

Hence $H$ grows linearly in both tails and has tail exponent $\beta=1$. The
objective is locally strongly convex but not globally strongly convex. This is
the $(m,\beta)=(2,1)$ regime: the local stationary scale is the quadratic
$\sqrt{\alpha}$ scale, while global recurrence is governed by the
subquadratic-tail geometry in the paper.

In this correctly specified one-dimensional experiment, write $p^\star$ for
the true win probability against the randomly drawn opponent and set

$$
A=\mathbb{E}\left[p^\star(1-p^\star)\right].
$$

For the skill error $e_t=\widehat{\theta}_t-\theta^\star$, linearization of the
logistic update at $\theta^\star$ gives

$$
e_{t+1}=(1-\alpha A)e_t+\alpha\xi_{t+1}+o(\alpha |e_t|),
$$

where $\xi_{t+1}$ is a martingale difference. Correct specification makes the
Hessian and score variance coincide:

$$
\mathbb{E}[\xi_{t+1}^2]
=\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{Var}(Y_{t+1}\mid p^\star)\right]
=A.
$$

The covariance $\Sigma$ of the $e_t/\sqrt{\alpha}$ diffusion limit therefore
solves the scalar Lyapunov equation

$$
A\Sigma+\Sigma A=A,
\qquad\text{hence}\qquad
\Sigma=\frac12.
$$

Thus $\mathrm{Var}(e_t)/\alpha\to 1/2$ as $\alpha\to0$, and the figure
uses $\mathrm{Var}(e_t)=\alpha/2$ as its reference line. The simulation
checks the local $m=2$ stationary scale. It does not estimate the fixed-step
contraction rate or test the $m>2$ non-Gaussian scaling results.

## 3. Dynamic Skill

The dynamic experiment observes binary outcomes against a known zero-skill
opponent. Latent focal-player skill follows one of three processes:

- `smooth`: a sinusoid with period $4\tau$;
- `ou`: a stationary Ornstein–Uhlenbeck discretization with persistence
  $\exp(-1/\tau)$; or
- `jump`: a new independent skill is drawn with probability $1/\tau$ per match.

For each process and each $\tau\in\lbrace 50,100,200,400,800\rbrace$, the
default build
uses 10 independent replications. Within a replication, all 29 values of
$\alpha$ see the same latent path and binary outcome stream, preserving common
random numbers across candidate steps. Replication seeds are generated
deterministically from master seed 20260820. The reported grid optimum minimizes
the mean post-burn-in tracking MSE across replications.

Scaling slopes use a local three-point quadratic interpolation of MSE on a
logarithmic $\alpha$ scale to avoid treating grid quantization as simulation
precision.
The 95% intervals resample the 10 replications within each $\tau$ 2,000 times
and refit the slope. They quantify Monte Carlo uncertainty conditional on this
simulation design. They are not confidence intervals for a theoretical
exponent and do not include finite-horizon, process-specification, or
asymptotic-approximation error. The complete simulation build takes about 18
seconds on the development machine with 10 replications.

The smooth and OU reference exponents, $-2/3$ and $-1/2$, come from the
corresponding tracking calculations in Aldous's sports-model analysis. Grid
resolution, finite horizons, and Monte Carlo error affect the fitted slopes.
No reference exponent is specified for the jump process.

## 4. ATP Data

Annual ATP workbooks for 2010–2025 are downloaded from
[Tennis-Data](http://www.tennis-data.co.uk/data.php). Raw files remain in the
ignored `data/cache/` directory.

Cleaning rules:

- keep rows marked `Completed`;
- remove retirements and walkovers;
- require a date and two distinct player names;
- orient players alphabetically, independently of the outcome;
- retain court surface, rank, and available decimal odds; and
- use a source-year/source-row identifier only for deterministic ordering.

The final sample contains 39,247 matches. The 2020 count is lower because the
professional tennis calendar was interrupted; the pipeline does not impute
missing events.

The workbooks contain dates but no start times. Predictions for every match on
one date are computed from the state at the beginning of that date. Updates are
then applied as a batch. This prevents the row order in a workbook from being
treated as the order in which matches occurred.

ATP ranking columns are retained in the cleaned in-memory records but are not
passed to any model. The Elo models use past outcomes and surface only.
De-vigged market odds are evaluated separately as an external benchmark.

## 5. Chronological Evaluation

The split is fixed before model comparison:

| period | purpose | matches |
|---|---|---:|
| 2010–2017 | initialize online states | 20,232 |
| 2018–2021 | select model and strategy parameters | 8,728 |
| 2022–2025 | final test | 10,287 |

All candidate models run from the first match. No state is reset at a split
boundary. Test outcomes do not affect model-family selection, update-factor
selection, overlay coefficients, or betting threshold selection.

The compared Elo models are:

- overall Elo with constant $K$;
- global-plus-surface Elo with separate global and surface steps; and
- an exponential-weights mixture of Elo experts with different $K$ values.

Probability forecasts are evaluated by log loss, Brier score, and calibration
error. Log loss is the primary selection criterion because it is a strictly
proper score and matches the online mixture objective.

On the locked test period, Surface Elo is compared directly with Overall Elo
using per-match paired log-loss differences. Calendar months are resampled as
blocks, preserving each match's two losses as a pair. A separate year-by-year
table reports the same comparison by calendar year. Neither output is used for
model or parameter selection.

## 6. Market Benchmark and Decision Rule

Pinnacle odds are used when both sides are present; otherwise the reported
bookmaker average is used. For decimal odds $o_a,o_b$, the de-vigged market
probability is

$$
p_a^{\mathrm{mkt}}=\frac{1/o_a}{1/o_a+1/o_b}.
$$

The market overlay is a two-parameter logit combination of the de-vigged
market and the validation-selected Elo model. Both coefficients are selected
on 2018–2021. The betting threshold is then selected on the same validation
period from a fixed grid. An explicit abstain policy is included in the choice
set.

For a selected side with model probability $p$ and decimal odds $o$, the
strategy requires positive expected value and a minimum probability edge. The
README reports unit ROI, which uses one unit per bet. Lower-level outputs also
contain a fractional-Kelly terminal bankroll and drawdown for compatibility.
These path statistics are not portfolio-risk estimates because the data do not
identify within-day order, simultaneous exposure, or when the quoted price was
available.

Uncertainty is estimated by resampling calendar months rather than individual
matches. The same block scheme is used for paired probability-column log-loss
differences and for strategy ROI.

## 7. Interpretation Limits

- The market odds timestamp and real-time attainability are not verified in
  the workbooks. A historical result at those prices is not an execution model.
- Limits, rejected bets, latency, market impact, taxes, and account constraints
  are omitted.
- Players, tournaments, and matches are dependent. Monthly resampling preserves
  temporal blocks but does not separately model player or tournament dependence.
- Model and strategy selection share the validation period. The test period is
  untouched, but further use of it would turn it into training data.
- The overlay's test log-loss difference and ROI interval both include zero.
  Neither result establishes a market edge.
- The simulations assume a correctly specified Bradley–Terry outcome model.
  Real tennis violates that assumption through injuries, scheduling, format,
  surface transitions, and other omitted information.
